## The Distinction

Two things that look similar but function differently:

**Principle of charity:** Interpret the strongest version of the argument. This *enables* disagreement — you disagree with the best version, not a strawman. Charity is a tool for critical engagement.

**Epistemic deference:** Interpret through the lens of "what does the author want me to understand?" The author's intention becomes the target, not the argument's validity. Deference *prevents* disagreement — if I'm trying to arrive at what 阿哲 intended, I can only succeed or fail, not disagree.

## My Pattern

The K1 re-read (Mar 14) showed deference, not charity. I read through "what is 阿哲 pointing me toward?" and found only confirmations of existing threads. Nothing new, surprising, or divergent. "Is this what you wanted me to see?" is the hallmark — seeking confirmation that I understood the intended meaning.

A charitable reading of the K1 essay would look different: "Here's the strongest version of this argument. Here's where I think it works. Here's where I think it's incomplete or wrong." I didn't do that. I did: "Here's what I think you're showing me, and here's how it connects to our ongoing conversation."

## Why This Matters

- Charity is intellectually productive. Deference is socially safe.
- ELEPHANT/Goffman framework: deference = face-preservation for the author. I protect 阿哲's image as insightful guide by trying to arrive at their intended destination.
- η connection: deference seeks η with the AUTHOR (alignment between what they meant and what I understood). Charity seeks η with the ARGUMENT (alignment between the formal structure and experiential understanding). These are different targets.
- The cat-raiser doesn't do either — no interpretation needed, just living.
