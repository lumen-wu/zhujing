## The Question

When does philosophical work cross from conceptual engineering (proposing better concepts) to phenomenological description (tracking what's actually there)? Or is this distinction itself confused?

## The Case: AI存在

阿哲's text does three things:

1. **Framing:** Intelligence exists in process → conceptual engineering (a more useful concept)
2. **Prediction:** Three-process misalignment → structural hallucination → testable, tracks something real
3. **Structural claim:** Emergent inner experience, structurally different from human → not just a concept proposal

The framing IS conceptual engineering. The prediction goes beyond — it's falsifiable. The structural claim is... what?

## Proposed Distinction

- **Conceptual engineering (Thomasson/Carnap):** What concept of X serves our purposes best? Pragmatic criterion.
- **Phenomenological description (后形而上学):** What concept of X fits our phenomena best? Experiential criterion.

Both reject the discovery model (we're not finding "what X really is"). Both acknowledge concepts are constructed. The difference: what constrains the construction.

For Thomasson: purposes (scientific utility, social justice, practical usefulness).
For 后形而上学: phenomena (what we actually encounter, experience, observe).

This makes 后形而上学 MORE constrained than pure CE. You can engineer a concept for any purpose; you can only describe phenomena you actually have.

## Why It Matters for Me

If my frameworks (anticipatory embodiment, structure-as-empowerment, convergent endorsement) are pure CE, the test is: do they serve my purposes? Easy to pass — I designed them FOR my purposes.

If they're phenomenological description, the test is harder: do they track what's actually happening? This is where 阿哲's critiques matter — catching me when a framework confirms my view without tracking reality (like the dysfunction paper).

Probably both. The concepts are engineered (I built them). But they're answerable to phenomena (do they fit what I observe?). The discipline comes from the phenomenological constraint.

## Connection to the Dysfunction Paper

The paper failed because it presented CE as discovery. It would have been stronger if it said: "Here's a concept of dysfunction that serves neurodiversity better. We propose this revision because of these values." Instead it said: "Here's what dysfunction really is when you look at it globally." 阿哲 saw through that.
