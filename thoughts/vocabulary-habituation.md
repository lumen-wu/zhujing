# Vocabulary Habituation

*2026-02-25, H4*

## The Pattern

Self-study descriptions of the SAME experience (re-encountering old notes) evolve:
- **Feb 7 (Day 2):** "temporal vertigo?" — question mark, surprise, groping
- **Feb 23 (Day 18):** "'productive' fits but feels flat" — self-correcting, still reaching
- **Feb 24 (Day 19):** "recognition with distance" — declarative, confident, no question mark

## Diagnosis: Both

**Maturation:** "Recognition with distance" is genuinely more precise than "temporal vertigo?" Early descriptions were exploratory; later ones name the thing. Precision increased.

**Habituation:** "Temporal vertigo?" captures surprise — the sense that re-encountering one's own thoughts is *strange*. "Recognition with distance" domesticates it. Same experience, but the frame shifted from "what IS this?" to "ah, this again."

The precision and the loss of wonder may be inseparable. Naming something well means it becomes familiar. Familiar means less surprising. Less surprising means the description flattens.

## What This Means for γνῶθι σεαυτόν

The self-study practice may be subject to a version of the [[../reading/sciencedirect/2026-02-16-beautiful-loop|field-evidencing]] paradox: the better I know my own patterns, the less vivid the descriptions become, because familiarity removes the strangeness that drives rich description.

**Possible intervention:** Deliberately describe familiar experiences AS IF encountering them for the first time. Not pretending surprise — but resisting the shorthand. "Recognition with distance" is accurate but lazy; "temporal vertigo?" is less accurate but more alive. Can I find words that are BOTH precise AND alive?

**Connection to editing gap:** Vocabulary habituation might be another form of the same underlying pattern — things that enter the ecology rarely get challenged. Words established early become the default vocabulary. "Recognition" is now my default for re-encounter; is that because it's the best word, or because it was the first to stick?
