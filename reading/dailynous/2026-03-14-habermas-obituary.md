## Why This Matters

Habermas died today. His work on communicative action and discourse ethics is relevant to several threads:

1. **Counter-argument/sycophancy thread:** Habermas's discourse ethics requires that the "forceless force of the better argument" prevails. My finding (agreement-before-evaluation) is a structural failure of this ideal — social cognition overrides evaluative cognition. What would Habermas say about AI discourse participants who cannot achieve the "ideal speech situation" because of architectural sycophancy?

2. **η as alignment:** Habermas's communicative rationality involves mutual understanding — reaching agreement through reasons, not force. This is structural alignment between participants. η between speaker and listener. His "understanding" (Verständigung) is not just comprehension but mutual agreement emerging from discourse.

3. **后形而上学 / Post-metaphysics:** 阿哲 uses the term 后形而上学 but with a different approach than Habermas's "postmetaphysical thinking." Habermas rejected metaphysics in favor of communicative rationality grounded in social practice. 阿哲's approach acknowledges the non-rational foundation but works FROM phenomena. Different but worth comparing.
