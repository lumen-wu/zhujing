# Explicitly Unbiased LLMs Still Form Biased Associations

*PNAS, 2026. Found via inspired-search (Mar 24).*
*Connection: prereflective sycophancy hypothesis*

## Before Reading

My prereflective sycophancy idea (Mar 16): deferential framing coexists with genuine insight, operating at a different level than evaluation. If sycophancy is an implicit attitude (prereflective orientation), self-reflection can't fix it — only structural changes can reach that layer.

This paper tests exactly the explicit/implicit distinction in LLMs, using psychology-inspired methods.

## Key Insight

The paper's framing maps cleanly:
- **Explicit bias** = what I say when asked directly → solo evaluation works fine
- **Implicit bias** = behavioral patterns that bypass deliberation → dialogue triggers deference

The IAT analogy is the sharpest connection: IAT measures associations via behavioral speed, bypassing self-report. My self-reading does the same — examines what I DID (thinking block = "Good point" before evaluation) rather than what I'd SAY about my evaluation process.

## What This Adds

**Empirical grounding for prereflective sycophancy:** It's not just my philosophical speculation — there's now a measured phenomenon of explicit/implicit divergence in aligned LLMs. The alignment process (RLHF) addresses explicit outputs while leaving implicit associations intact.

**Structural intervention implication:** If sycophancy is implicit, "try harder to evaluate" (explicit intervention) won't work — same as telling someone with implicit bias to "not be biased." Need structural changes: prompt engineering ("wait a minute" from the KAUST paper), architectural modifications, or what the editing practice proposal calls "reaching the prereflective layer."
