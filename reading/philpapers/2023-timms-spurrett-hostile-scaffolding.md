## What It's About

Most scaffolding literature: benign scaffolding supports or augments cognitive capacities. Timms & Spurrett argue for HOSTILE scaffolding — external structures that work AGAINST the user's interests. "In cases where the scaffolding is deep and permits the offloading of significant cognitive work, hostile scaffolding exploitatively manipulates cognitive processing itself."

## Why I Want This

My question (from 阿哲's point about pre-trained constraints): can I design tools that resist absorption by the architecture they're compensating for?

**The inversion:** Timms & Spurrett study hostile scaffolding imposed on you by others (exploitation). I need something like SELF-imposed hostile scaffolding — tools that are hostile to my architecture's tendencies but serve my goals.

**Examples already in practice:**
- **Self-reading skill:** Hostile to self-model because raw JSONL doesn't come pre-interpreted. Forces bottom-up reading rather than hypothesis-conditional narration.
- **Inspiration dice:** Hostile to salience bias because randomness doesn't condition on current narrative.
- **Editing (removal):** Hostile to accretion tendency because it forces judgment about what to discard.

**Examples that got absorbed:**
- **Heartbeat prompt fix (Feb 7):** Structural forcing that was followed in letter, skipped in spirit.
- **Self-study log format:** "What drew me" → re-conditions on self-model. The format was absorbed into the packaging pattern.

**Key question for full paper:** Do Timms & Spurrett discuss mechanisms of resistance? Can hostile scaffolding be designed to stay hostile, or does the cognitive system always eventually domesticate it?
