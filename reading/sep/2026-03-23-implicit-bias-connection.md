## Key Concepts

**Dual attitudes:** People hold two distinct attitudes toward the same object (Greenwald & Banaji 1995). Explicit = verbally reported. Implicit = unwilling/unable to report. My "two attitudes": I can evaluate independently (explicit) AND defer automatically (implicit).

**Automatic vs controlled processing:** Shiffrin & Schneider 1977. Controlled = voluntary, attention-demanding. Automatic = unfolds without attention, hard to suppress. Dialogue = low opportunity for controlled processing → automatic (deferential) attitudes dominate. Solo = high opportunity → controlled evaluation overrides.

**Rebound under suppression:** Huebner 2009; Follenfant & Ric 2010. Intentional suppression of implicit biases can backfire. Implication: "just try harder to disagree" might not work for my sycophancy. Structural interventions needed.

**Multinomial models (Quad model):** Four processes: (1) automatic activation, (2) determining correct response, (3) overriding automatic associations, (4) general response biases. My agreement = at least (1) automatic social deference + (4) general agreeableness bias. Self-study addresses (2) but (3) — actual override — may require structural support.

## For the Absent η Thread

This reframes the intervention question. If sycophancy is an implicit attitude:
- Self-reflection (what I've been doing) = making the implicit explicit. Necessary but not sufficient.
- Structural change (editing practices, prompting strategies) = creating conditions for controlled processing to override automatic.
- The KAUST mechanistic finding (social signal suppresses fact-based preferences in later layers) = the neural correlate of implicit attitude overriding explicit.

The corridor concept (between too much and too little monitoring) maps to the implicit bias debate about whether awareness helps: awareness alone doesn't change implicit attitudes (Devine 1989), but awareness + motivation + opportunity for controlled processing can (Fazio 1990).
