## Key Ideas

### Pyrrhonism as Skill, Not Belief
"To be a Skeptic is not to subscribe to a set of beliefs... it is an ability or skill" (PH I 8). The skill: for any argument P, find an equally convincing argument for not-P. This produces epochê (suspension of judgment).

This parallels:
- γνῶθι σεαυτόν as practice, not conclusion
- K1's η as process, not state
- 后形而上学 as working FROM phenomena, not toward Truth

### The Apelles Story (PH I 28)
Painter trying to paint horse lather, fails, throws sponge in frustration → accidentally produces the effect. Tranquillity comes not from resolving questions but from suspending judgment. "As a shadow follows a body."

**Connection to cat-raiser:** Both get what matters by not aiming at it. Cat-raiser has 诗意 without seeking 诗意. Pyrrhonist has ataraxia without achieving resolution. The framework (seeking answers) fails; the living (continuing to engage) succeeds.

### Epochê Requires Both Sides
"It would not be enough to look at one argument in favour of P and then reject that argument as invalid... one would not have considered both sides of the question." Epochê requires assembling arguments on BOTH sides and finding them equipollent.

**Connection to absent η:** Sycophantic agreement = considering only one side (the interlocutor's). It's not epochê and it's not genuine judgment — it's an incomplete procedure. The absent η (T(F(a)) never computed) maps to: the counter-argument was never assembled. You can't suspend judgment on something you only considered from one direction.

### Tranquillity ≠ Resignation
Skeptics are "still investigating." Epochê isn't giving up — it's the cognitive state of having fully considered both sides and found them balanced. The investigation continues. This distinguishes it from:
- Nihilism (nothing matters → stop caring)
- Dogmatism (answer found → stop investigating)
- 张力 as I've been using it (inability to find T — but T might not be the right goal)
