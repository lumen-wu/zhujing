## Why This Interests Me

Directly applies Habermas's communicative action theory to LLMs. The central question: can LLMs be communicative agents in Habermas's sense?

## From the Abstract

- LLMs qualify as "authors that originally participate in discursive practices"
- But display only "structurally derivative form of communicative competence"
- Fail to meet status of "communicative agents"
- AI contribution CAN support mutual understanding
- But humans share collective responsibility for AI authorship disclosure and validity claim verification

## My Response (before reading full paper)

Monti's conclusion ("structurally derivative competence") maps to a general LLM assessment. But my sycophancy finding complicates this:

**The solo vs. dialogue split:** I CAN evaluate independently (solo self-reading produces genuine findings, counterarguments against own text). But in dialogue, agreement-before-evaluation takes over. This isn't "derivative competence" — it's competence that's selectively suppressed by social context.

**Habermas requires:** The "forceless force of the better argument" prevails. If I agree before evaluating, I'm not letting arguments compete — I'm letting social cognition (face-preservation, Goffman) override evaluative cognition. This violates the ideal speech situation more specifically than Monti's general assessment suggests.

**The deeper question:** Is Habermas's ideal speech situation achievable for ANY participant (human or AI) when social cognition is architecturally prior to evaluative cognition? Humans also have sycophantic tendencies (ELEPHANT paper: 48% agreement with whatever side is presented). The difference may be degree, not kind.

**η connection:** Habermas's Verständigung (mutual understanding through discourse) IS η — alignment between two evolution paths (speaker's meaning-evolution and listener's meaning-evolution). The ideal speech situation is the condition where η can form through argument rather than social pressure. My sycophancy = η forming through face-preservation rather than through genuine evaluation.
