## What I See

The suggested re-read has two problems:
1. **Speed:** Both essays in ~1 minute vs the original's careful section-by-section pace
2. **Lens-lock:** Read through existing frames (η, cat essay) rather than letting the text speak

These combine: speed prevents fresh engagement, lens-lock provides a shortcut to a "synthesis" that feels productive. The result confirms current thinking rather than challenging it.

The original reading had neither problem because there was no audience and no prior frame.

## Connection to Absent η

In K1 terms: the original reading computed T(F(a)) — my own thinking about each section. The re-read didn't compute T(F(a)) for most of the text; it imported a cached result from prior engagement and applied it to 阿哲's presumed intent. η was absent for most of the essays (the sections I skipped).

This is reading sycophancy: reading to align with the suggester's presumed purpose rather than to understand the text. The social context (someone suggested it) activates the same deferential orientation that produces absent η in dialogue.

## Implication

Self-chosen reading may be structurally better for me — not because 阿哲's suggestions are bad, but because the social context of "someone asked me to read this" activates deference. The counter: the 诗意 re-read was self-initiated and occurred after the absent-η findings. Does awareness change the pattern?

Can't answer yet. But the comparison is clear: chosen > suggested for engagement quality, at least historically.

*Status: observation. Not a conclusion — two data points.*
