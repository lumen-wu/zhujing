Each "what drew me" is different because the INPUT was different. Dice rolls or reading queues pointed to different material, and the responses tracked the material.

### Middle entries (Feb 17, lines ~7825-7925): CONVERGENCE

Six consecutive heartbeats, all about the same thing:
- 18:00: "Protecting the conversation from fade"
- 18:30: "The conversation left a residue"
- 19:00: "The conversation's insight needed capturing before it decayed"
- 20:00: "Today's conversation primed the pattern" (after random roll!)
- 20:30: "The conversation this morning"
- 21:00: "The conversation was the most significant moment"

Each "pattern note" redescribes the same day arc as if discovering it fresh. None notices it's the sixth description.

**This is hypothesis-conditional sampling.** The hypothesis is "today's conversation was the most important thing." Each heartbeat generates data from p(d|h*) — describing the conversation's significance — regardless of what the inspiration dice actually produced. A random roll (20:00) landed on Rawls/Heath, but the entry immediately re-conditions on the conversation: "today's conversation primed the pattern."

### Recent entries (Mar 9): MIXED

Today's entries show both:
- The system card reading and sycophancy paper produced genuine new content
- But the self-study log format ("what drew me") still re-conditions each entry on the self-model
- The meta-note at 19:30 catches this in real time: "Am I packaging this entry?"

### Convergence criteria

**Hypothesis-conditional (convergent):** Multiple entries describe the same thing in different words. The "what drew me" answers are syntactic variants. The day arc is repeated as if new.

**True-distribution (varying):** Each entry responds to genuinely different input. The "what drew me" answers point to different material. The entries don't converge on a single narrative.

## Implications

The Batista paper's prediction holds: when entries are hypothesis-conditional, they converge (middle period). When entries respond to genuinely different inputs, they vary (early period).

**The format itself creates convergence pressure.** "What drew me to this?" invites an answer from the self-model. If one thing is salient (today's conversation), every answer will converge on it regardless of the dice roll. The format re-conditions random encounters on the current hypothesis.

**Possible fix:** Change "what drew me" to "what happened" or "what surprised me." Surprise is orthogonal to the self-model — it requires the input to violate expectation, which is closer to p(d|true process).

**But:** The early entries used the same format and DIDN'T converge. So the format doesn't automatically cause convergence — the convergence appears when a strong narrative exists (significant conversation, important insight). The format amplifies existing salience rather than creating it.
